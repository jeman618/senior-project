Description

The strawberry is a widely cultivated fruit that many people enjoy. It is known for its bright red color, juicy texture, and sweet flavor. They are usually eaten fresh, or frozen, or dried. They can be eaten by themselves and can be added to many types of foods, such as smoothies, yogurts, desserts, jams, drinks, and more. You can buy them at grocery stores, farmers markets, and even grow them in your own garden! If you want to grow them, here is what you need to know.

How to Grow

Strawberries should be planted in growing zones 3-10 according to the USDA. They should be planted in spring in zone 6 and northward, and in fall in zone 7 and southward. It is best to grow them in raised beds or containers that have a depth of at least 8 inches to ensure good drainage and to prevent root rot.

When you plant them, make sure to choose a location that gets plenty of sunlight and has well-drained soil. They need at least 8 hours of direct sunlight daily. Give them room to grow and space them around 18 inches apart. The soil should be acidic and have a pH between 5.5 and 6.8. Give them 1-1.5 inches of water per week, but water the soil directly. Avoid wetting the leaves to prevent disease.

Harvesting

Expect strawberries to be ready during the Summer, but always wait until they are fully ripe to pick. If it is still white or green, it is not ready to be harvested. The berry should be fully red and have a glossy appearance. Pick them in the morning when they are cool because that is when they are firmer and higher quality. Gently pull the berry from the stem, leaving the green cap attached. Be careful not to damage the plant when harvesting by squeezing too hard. After harvesting, store the strawberries in a container if you are going to eat them now or refrigerate them to keep them fresh. Wash them just before eating, not before storing, to remove any dirt or debris.

Fun Fact

Strawberries are not true berries! They are classified as aggregate fruits because they form from multiple flowers on a single plant.
